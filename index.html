Overview

The simulator represents a cloth as point masses joined by springs, advances their positions over time, resolves collisions, and renders the result with programmable shaders.

Part 1: Masses and springs

The grid stores point masses in row-major order. Structural springs connect adjacent horizontal and vertical points, shearing springs connect diagonal neighbors, and bending springs connect points two grid steps apart. Selected grid points are pinned.

Without shearing, the visible springs run along grid rows and columns. Shearing alone forms diagonal crosses, while enabling all three types combines both patterns.

Part 2: Numerical integration

The simulation accumulates external and Hooke spring forces, uses damped Verlet integration for unpinned masses, and limits spring extension to 110% of rest length by moving the endpoints. Bending springs use one fifth of the base spring constant.

Lower spring stiffness permits more deformation under load; higher stiffness resists it. Increasing density increases the gravitational force and inertia of each mass, producing more sag and changing the motion. In the higher-density trial, visible transient wrinkles later smoothed out. Damping reduces the velocity term at each step: more damping suppresses oscillations sooner, whereas less damping lets them persist longer. The damping images show two moments from the higher-damping run; they do not by themselves measure settling time against the default. The sphere comparison in Part 3 also shows the effect of ks = 500, 5000, and 50000.

Part 3: Collisions with objects

For a point inside a sphere, the sphere collision handler finds the radial surface point and applies a friction-scaled correction from the previous position. For a plane, it detects when the point crosses sides and projects it to the original side with a small offset, applying friction to the correction.

At ks = 500, the cloth forms narrower, more irregular folds than at ks = 5000, where the folds appear broader and more regular. At ks = 50000, the drape holds a wider shape with fewer, broader folds.

Part 4: Self-collisions

The simulation places masses in spatial buckets and checks candidates in its own bucket and neighboring buckets. When two masses are closer than twice the cloth thickness, it accumulates a separating displacement and applies the average, scaled by the number of simulation steps. Hash collisions are filtered by checking actual distances.

The cloth first bunches at the ground, then forms several stacked folds that spread and flatten over time.

At the lower density, the captured cloth has a broad, smooth fold compared with the default-density sequence. At the lower spring constant, the captured folds are finer and more irregular. These images show particular moments in a moving simulation, so differences in timing may also affect their appearance.

Part 5: Shaders

A vertex shader transforms vertices and passes their properties to the rasterizer; a fragment shader computes color from the interpolated properties. Blinn–Phong combines ambient light, diffuse illumination based on the surface-to-light angle, and a view-dependent specular highlight based on the halfway vector. The texture shader samples the image using interpolated UV coordinates. Bump mapping changes the shading normal from height-map gradients; displacement mapping additionally moves vertices, so its silhouette depends on mesh resolution. The mirror shader reflects the view ray and samples a cubemap.

The ambient component is faint and uniform; diffuse lighting reveals the orientation of the folds; the specular term isolates the narrow highlights. The combined view adds all three.

Both mappings use the non-default texture_3 height map. Bump mapping changes the lighting without changing the outline; displacement also shifts vertices, visibly changing the cloth edge. Their scales differ to make each effect visible.

The coarse sphere has a more faceted outline. The fine sphere has a smoother outline and more vertices available for displacement. Bump mapping changes shading at both resolutions but cannot change the silhouette.

Extra credit: Wind

A toggle enables a spatially varying wind. Each triangle receives pressure proportional to its area and the signed square of the wind component along its normal, divided among its three point masses. The field varies with position, so different parts of the cloth receive different forces.

Both views use density 15 g/cm², ks = 5000, and damping 0.2%. With wind enabled, additional rippling is visible near the lower edge; the change is subtle from this angle.

Extra credit: Custom shader

The custom material combines a procedural woven pattern, Blinn–Phong lighting, and a subtle view-dependent environment reflection. The pattern is generated from the surface UV coordinates; it does not require a separate texture image.
